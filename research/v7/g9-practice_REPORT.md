# Practitioner sweep: what competition winners actually did (2026-11-07)

Role: Kaggle write-ups, forecasting-competition solutions, blogs, Stack Exchange, and a few textbooks practitioners use. Academic variants (BOCPD papers, Fixed-Share theory, logit-normal priors) are left to g8. No scorer was run. Every point estimate below is a translation of a published trick onto the published v6 score, not a new validation number.

Bar: pair mixer v6 at **56.29**. Loss ratios stable .054, short_history .105, drift .049, shared_change .056. Raw errors .0106 / .0186 / .0093 / .0116. Mean ratio R = 0.066, and 60/(1+R) = 56.29. Differentiating, **one condition's ratio moving by 0.01 is about +0.13 points**. A +0.40 point ship needs about 0.030 off one ratio, or about 0.008 off all four. Noise between episode halves is about ±0.4. Short history is the only condition whose raw error is far from the others (.0186 vs ~.010). Drift is already the best raw error; it is not where the points are.

v6 already does several things this literature would recommend, so they are not re-recommended: three half-lives (12/40/100), a Beta-mixture win prior whose posterior mean moves with the cell counts, size-mode Bayes with `k_size=16` and `k_machine=32`, `mode_temp`, online expert weights, pair rediscovery in `adapt()`, and episode-level cross-fitting. Fixed-Share (`alpha`), exact static Bayes, logistic/trees, CTW/PPM/GRU, and global prior-centre tweaks were tried and lost or tied (FINDINGS, h1). `decay_missing` defaults to **True** and `adapt()` does not override it, while the code comment says False is what keeps the 24 sampled older ticks alive across the gap. That one bit is unchecked in this sweep; it is step zero of idea 1, not a new model.

## Top 10

### 1. Fit the mixer a second time on histories that have been masked the way short_history is masked, and dispatch

**Sources.** Arthur Suilin, [1st place, Web Traffic Time Series Forecasting](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/web-traffic-time-series-forecasting/writeups/arthur-suilin-1st-place-solution): the network never saw a full series. It trained on random fixed-length crops, which he describes as the augmentation that mattered. OS, [8th place, same competition](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/web-traffic-time-series-forecasting/writeups/os-8th-place-with-kalman-filters): a single statistic (year-on-year self-similarity) chose Kalman versus a median-of-medians fallback, per series, with no learned stacker. John Termaat, [10th, Rock-Paper-Scissors](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rock-paper-scissors/writeups/john-termaat-10th-place-layered-multi-window-count): a slow offline pass counted which windows won against logged opponents, and the shipped agent kept only those windows.

**What they did.** They trained the predictor on the observation process it would face at test time, then switched policies with a dumb feature of the series, not with a second model class.

**Map.** Short history is the worst slice (.105, raw .0186). A drop of that ratio from .105 to .075 is about **+0.40 points**; to .060 (in line with the other three) is about **+0.59**. g4 says short-history *steady* error is 62% size. The last 40 ticks are contiguous, so lags 1/2/4 are observed at the query; the damage is the gap and the 24-tick sample, which change how much mass the half-life-100 expert and `k_machine=32` still carry. v6's half-lives, `k_size`, `k_machine` and `decay_missing=True` were chosen on ordinary histories (`tune_pair_mixer.py`, both-halves rule in the constructor comment). `adapt()` refits pairs and cell mode on full train histories only.

**Do this.** (a) Cross-fit `decay_missing=False` against the shipped default, scored on short-history episodes only. It is already implemented. (b) If that is not enough, build masked copies of train: keep the last 40 ticks before each cutoff and 24 older ticks sampled the way the kit samples them, refit half-lives and the two size pseudocounts on those copies, and at predict time use the masked config when the history has a gap of that shape. Do not retune on validation episodes.

**Round-two safe.** Yes. The mask is a property of the condition, re-estimated if round two changes the 40+24 pattern; read the pattern off the new kit rather than hard-coding 40 and 24. **Cost.** Low for (a), a few hours for (b), stdlib, inside the 30-minute predict budget because the mask is applied at fit time. **Expected gain.** +0.1 to +0.5 if the shortfall is the train/test observation mismatch; near 0 if `decay_missing=False` was already rejected on a cross-fit this sweep could not see. This is the only idea whose published analogue lines up with a slice big enough to clear the +0.4 ship bar by itself.

### 2. On a shared flip, drop pre-break counts. Do not share weight on every tick

**Sources.** Santa 2020 discussion, [estimating thresholds under decay](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/santa-2020/discussion/216425): a small error in the *initial* threshold, multiplied by decay, was worse than estimating the *current* threshold directly. The same post updates a Beta with discounted wins and also bumps a bandit when the *opponent* pulls it. A River write-up of a synthetic reversal, [Online ML on streaming data](https://codezup.com/implementing-online-machine-learning-for-real-time-streaming-data/): when the new rule is the inverse of the old one, a static model falls to ~0.43 and stays there; a model that already discounts recovers; an ADWIN reset fired 47 steps late and added only about one point of accuracy, while a second seed false-alarmed. River's own wrapper, [`DriftRetrainingClassifier`](https://github.com/online-ml/river/blob/330a2505/river/drift/retrain.py), either resets or swaps in a shadow model that has been training since a *warning*, not since the start of the stream. Gregory Gundersen's [BOCD walkthrough](https://gregorygundersen.com/blog/2019/08/13/bocd/) is the clean statement of what a run length is: the predictive distribution is a mixture over "how long ago the parameter last changed," and a run length of 0 throws the old sufficient statistics away.

**What they did.** Abrupt, shared changes were handled by deleting old counts, using other series as the trigger. Continuous down-weighting was enough for gradual change and was not improved much by a jittery detector.

**Map.** Shared change is the second-worst raw error (.0116, ratio .056). FINDINGS: one abrupt direction flip on all 8 machines, near 0.64 of the cutoff, pair and size unchanged; 16/24 episodes flip all 8. An exponential half-life of 100 never assigns zero weight to pre-flip spins, so the slow expert keeps voting for the old polarity. Fixed-Share was already tried and hurt, which matches the River result: a detector that fires on stable episodes taxes the best slice. Drift is gradual and already the best raw error (.0093); a reset is the wrong tool there, and a parametric ramp (PLAN item 4) is the drift tool.

**Do this.** Per machine, a one-bit residual: recent parity agreement minus earlier agreement, on the pair the fast expert currently likes. If at least 6 of 8 machines break inside a short window, zero the half-life-100 counts from before that window and leave half-lives 12 and 40 alone. Fit the vote threshold and the window on train, by episode cross-fit. Require the gain on shared_change episodes and no loss on stable. A shadow copy of the slow expert that only starts accumulating at the warning (River's background model) is the same idea with a softer swap.

**Round-two safe.** Yes, if the threshold is refit. A hard-coded "0.64 of the cutoff" is not safe. **Cost.** An afternoon, stdlib, no new dependency. **Expected gain.** +0.05 to +0.20 if most of the shared-change gap is stale slow-expert mass. Not enough to ship alone. Do not expect a drift gain; a false alarm on stable gives the gain back.

### 3. Search the size-atom rule offline, average the near-ties, ship a table

**Sources.** Icecuber, [1st, Abstraction and Reasoning Challenge](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/abstraction-and-reasoning-challenge/writeups/icecuber-1st-place-solution-code-and-official-docu): each task is a new hidden rule. He enumerates a DSL at *test* time, ranks programs by how many training examples they fit, then by fewest transformations, then by fewest pieces, and keeps the top 3. Diagonal flips, a pure data augmentation, moved him from 17 to 21 solved tasks, more than the jump from search depth 3 to 4. [2nd place, Conway's Reverse Game of Life 2020](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/conways-reverse-game-of-life-2020/writeups/under-a-penny-2nd-place-solution-write-up-team-und) (ebouteillon / Under a penny): SAT solves some boards perfectly and runs forever on others, so they ran a genetic algorithm and a SAT solver side by side and kept the best feasible board inside the time limit. [6th place, same competition](https://github.com/cjm715/kaggle-game-of-life) (cjm715): score a candidate predecessor by rolling it *forward* and comparing to the observed board.

**What they did.** They treated "infer the generator, then predict" as a search over a small program class, scored by reconstruction, with a complexity tie-break and a clock. They did not memorize the public boards.

**Map.** Size is 40% of v6's squared error and the last ~1.6 points once win probability is perfect (g4: perfect win probability scores 58.44). The atom is not fixed per machine (that plug-in scored 53.88). Lags 1/2/4 do not explain the switch either. g5 is looking for the rule. The failure mode already observed, in c1, is a size posterior that is confidently wrong. ARC submits a vote over the top 3 because the metric is exact-match; our metric is squared error against the true distribution, so a near-tie must be **averaged**, not argmaxed. `size_mode_mixture` already averages two *modes* given counts. It does not average two *switching rules*.

**Do this.** At rebuild time, on train histories only, enumerate a short list of switching rules (which lags, which function of big-win bits, whether the bit is per machine or per type). Score each rule by the Brier of the resulting size distribution on a held-out half of episodes. Keep every rule within the noise of the best (±0.4 points, or a paired interval that covers 0). Ship the mixture of those rules' size distributions, weights proportional to exp(−held-out Brier), with a penalty for extra lags so a 3-lag rule has to win by more than noise. Predict time only looks up the table. If the search has not finished, ship v6's current size head. That is the Conway time-box.

**Round-two safe.** Yes, because the table is rebuilt from the new train and the new target atoms. Hard-coding main-round lags is not safe. **Cost.** The search is the expensive part. It has to finish inside the round-two window (90 min) or be a main-round overnight job whose *code* reruns next round. Predict time stays cheap. **Expected gain.** The ceiling is about +1.6 if the rule is real and stable across rounds. A wrong confident rule is how c1 lost two points. The average-the-ties constraint is what makes the search shippable. If g5 finds no rule that wins on both halves, this idea's output is "keep v6's size head," which is the correct output.

### 4. Any blend of v6 and the filter gets shrunk toward v6. Hill-climbing the weight is how blends overfit

**Sources.** Smith and Wallis, [A simple explanation of the forecast combination puzzle](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1468-0084.2008.00541.x) (2009): estimated combination weights lose to an equal average because the weight estimate is noisy; they also recommend ignoring error covariances, which add more noise than signal. Blanc and Setzer, [When to choose the simple average](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbrese/v69y2016i10p3951-3962.html): a decision rule based on sample size and how fast the optimal weight moves. The 50-year review [Forecast combinations](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.04216v2) repeats the same empirical fact. Hyndman's FFORMA, [second in M4](https://robjhyndman.com/publications/fforma/), is the exception that shows the boundary: a feature-based weight model beat a simple average by a lot *and* beat hard selection by about 10% on OWA, but only because no single method dominated (about 60% of series had one clear winner, 40% wanted equal weights). Chris Deotte, [hill-climbing post-mortem](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s4e9/writeups/chris-deotte-81st-place-stack-regression-over-clas): tolerance 1e-5 fit the tenth significant digit, improved CV and the public board, and **hurt the private board** relative to stopping at the metric's real resolution. The [`hillclimbers` README](https://github.com/Matt-OP/hillclimbers) quotes the practical rule: small train, positive weights only. Caruana's ensemble-selection experiments, [Cornell PDF](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/a161f0f7-1de0-4bb4-b03b-67ece6e1d566/content): with a 100-point hill-climb set, bagging the selection is the difference between a useful ensemble and noise.

**What they did.** They refused to trust a weight fit on a short validation set. Where the component forecasts were similar, they shipped the incumbent. Where they differed by series, they fit weights on features and still averaged rather than switching.

**Map.** g3: every 80/20 and 50/50 blend with v6 was worse than v6. g2's filter wins stable (.046 vs .054) and loses short and drift, and scores 55.47 overall. Arithmetic on the published ratios: replacing v6 on *all* of stable and nowhere else, with zero damage, changes mean R by 0.008/4 = 0.002 and the score by about **+0.11**. That is inside the ±0.4 noise. PLAN's "+0.2 to +0.8 from a selector" is only available if the selector also repairs short or shared change, which the filter does not. FFORMA's "selection loses to averaging" does **not** transfer: v6 dominates three conditions, so a soft blend reintroduces the filter's short-history damage (.162 vs .105). The combination puzzle says the opposite of "average them."

**Do this.** Keep g6's selector, but constrain it. Default weight on v6 is 1. The filter may receive weight only in a bin defined by train-only features (long history, high fraction of ticks consistent with one pair, no peer-break vote). Fit that one weight on one episode half, score on the other, and shrink it toward 0 by the usual normal-normal factor n/(n+k) with k large enough that a one-half fluke cannot move it. No negative weights, no 1e-5 tolerance, no third model. Ship only if both halves are positive and the paired interval sits above 0. Otherwise ship pure v6.

**Round-two safe.** Yes if the bin and the weight are refit on the new train. **Cost.** Small once g6's filter forecasts exist. **Expected gain.** 0 to +0.15. The value of this idea is mostly the points it refuses to give back.

### 5. Until a size cell has enough wins, predict the type's empirical size distribution and nothing else

**Sources.** GoodsForecast, [2nd, M5 Uncertainty](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207022000541): a weighted histogram of the series itself, then a median shift so the histogram agrees with a point forecast. They say the histogram, not the GBM, carried the distribution. Ouranos, [3rd, same track](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/m5-forecasting-uncertainty/writeups/ouranos-3rd-place-solution): at the noisiest level (item-store) the winning mix was about 0.2×model quantiles + 0.7×empirical sales quantiles over the last year and the last month, and a smaller weight on the model where the series was short. Walsmart, [6th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/m5-forecasting-uncertainty/writeups/walsmart-6th-solution-pure-statistical-forecast): a two-state (active/inactive) state-space model with a discrete emission, Monte Carlo at the bottom level, and a deliberately white box so the score stayed stable across years. The public Web Traffic kernel [median of Fibonacci windows](https://www.kaggle.com/code/safavieh/median-estimation-by-fibonacci-et-al-lb-44-9) is the same instinct for a single noisy series: several windows, then a median, because any one window is a fluke.

**What they did.** When the series was short they trusted a pooled or smoothed histogram more than the parametric head, and they put a number on that distrust (0.7 empirical at the leaf).

**Map.** v6 already shrinks size counts toward a type prior with `k_size=16` and mixes two atoms with `mode_temp=1`. Sixteen pseudo-counts is a lot when a dead steady machine in a 40-tick window produces a handful of wins, and it is also enough for one lucky mode to dominate the log-likelihood and spike the posterior. c1's size posterior being confidently wrong is this failure at the extreme. g4: short-history steady is 62% size. A global change to `k_size` is the "small prior tweak" bucket and should not be the experiment. A **floor** is different: below n wins in the active size cell, the predictive size distribution is exactly the type histogram from `fit()`, whose weights are already learned from train in `PairMixer.fit`.

**Do this.** Cross-fit one integer n0 per type (or one shared n0). Below n0 observed wins in the cell that the size head is about to use, emit `type_size_priors` (renormalized onto the two target atoms, which `adapt()` already knows how to read). Above n0, use today's mixture. Pick n0 on train halves.

**Round-two safe.** Yes. Atoms and the type histogram are re-read from the new targets and the new train. **Cost.** An hour. **Expected gain.** +0.05 to +0.25, concentrated on short history and on dead machines. Ceiling is well under the +1.6 of a true switching rule (idea 3); this is the hedge if idea 3 finds nothing.

### 6. Round-two runbook: freeze the knobs on a rehearsal shift, refit on all of the new train, and bail out on the clock

**Sources.** Evgeniia Grigoreva, [8th, Jane Street Real-Time Market Data Forecasting](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/jane-street-real-time-market-data-forecasting/writeups/evgeniia-grigoreva-private-lb-8th-solution): two time-series folds whose validation length matched the public set, plus a second test of the first-fold model on the last 200 dates **with a 200-day gap**, built to imitate the private set. She then retrained on every date up to the end, for the number of epochs chosen on CV, because a holdout that keeps the latest data out of the final fit is leaving signal behind. hyd, [1st, Optiver Trading at the Close](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/optiver-trading-at-the-close/discussion/487446), as summarized by [Insight Edge](https://techblog.insightedge.jp/entry/kaggle-optiver-solutions) and the [corrected notes](https://github.com/yuvi17064021/Feature-Engineering-Ideas-at-Optiver-Trading-at-Close.-/blob/main/2nd%20Part_DL_optiver_solution.md): one chronological holdout (400 days / 81 days), online refits every 12 days, and a runtime guard that **skipped the fifth update** when the notebook would have timed out. Four updates shipped; the fifth would have zeroed the submission. G-Research's [host write-up](https://www.gresearch.com/news/wrapping-up-the-g-research-crypto-forecasting-competition/): all three podium teams used LightGBM and said feature engineering, not the model class, decided it. Nathaniel Maddux, [2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/g-research-crypto-forecasting/writeups/nathaniel-maddux-2nd-place-solution), via the [notes collected here](https://kaggle.curtischong.me/competitions/G-Research-Crypto-Forecasting): walk-forward folds with a one-week embargo so the model cannot cheat across the cut, then train the submitted model with **no gap**, up to the last point. Tom Forbes, [13th, and 1st six weeks in](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/g-research-crypto-forecasting/writeups/tom-forbes-13th-place-final-1st-place-6-weeks-in-f): he split a target that silently changed definition when a row was missing, and trained separate models on the two definitions. Yeonjun In, [1st, M5 Accuracy](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/m5-forecasting-accuracy/writeups/yeonjun-in-stu-1st-place-solution): he picked the final ensemble by the **standard deviation** of scores across time folds and the public board, not by the best single fold, and shipped an equal average of six models trained at three pooling levels. The write-up of that method is [In, Jung and Lim](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v38y2022i4p1386-1399.html).

**What they did.** They rehearsed the private regime with a gap, froze every choice the rehearsal made, refit those frozen choices on all labeled data, and had an explicit abort when the clock ran out. They averaged a few precommitted variants with equal weights when that lowered the variance across folds.

**Map.** `adapt()` already rediscovers pairs, rediscovers size keys, and picks `cell_mode` by **in-sample** train cutoff Brier between two candidates. Two candidates is a small overfit, but it is the wrong template for ideas 1, 2 and 5, which have more knobs. Round two is 90 minutes, then a 30-minute Docker predict with no network. The Optiver failure mode is the one to design against: a rebuild that almost finishes and then emits nothing.

**Do this.** One script, already the PLAN item 0 shape. (1) Run the eval suite `round2_shift` preset before trusting any new knob; that preset is the Jane Street gap. (2) Choose knobs by episode cross-fit, requiring both halves non-negative against v6, which is Yeonjun's "watch the std" rule in a two-fold form. (3) Refit the frozen knobs on all of the new train, including the half you validated on. (4) If the clock passes a precommitted minute, write the previous zip and stop. (5) Log the discovered pairs. If they are not a permutation of the three disjoint pairs the kit uses, the run is in the Forbes situation (the target definition changed) and the human should look before upload. Do not add a third model class Sunday morning. G-Research's podium spent their time on the features they already understood.

**Round-two safe.** This is the safety work. **Cost.** The script is the cost; the judgments above are a page of rules. **Expected gain.** No main-round points. It is what stops a −2 to −12 repeat of static Bayes if round two moves the pairs or the mask.

### 7. Do not recalibrate v6, and do not stack on the validation episodes

**Sources.** Quantix Bio Labs, [post hoc recalibration can make risk models worse](https://labs.quantixmind.com/papers/p1/): with 25 calibration rows, Platt, beta and isotonic **raised** error on a model that was already roughly calibrated; isotonic was the worst (Brier 0.163 → 0.191 on one dataset); harm crossed zero only around 25–45 minority-class events, and for a *known* miscalibration of 0.05 the break-even was ~100 rows (Platt) and ~200 (isotonic). A perfectly calibrated simulator still got worse. Abzu's [calibration note](https://www.abzu.ai/data-science/calibration-introduction-part-2/) and Ojeda et al., [Statistics in Medicine](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.9921): beta calibration is the least-bad parametric map, and even it loses to refitting the original model when the calibration set is small. The survey [arxiv 2601.19944](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.19944) finds Venn-ABERS and beta help log-loss in aggregate and still records large negative tails. [arxiv 2002.10199](https://arxiv.org/html/2002.10199v2) puts the nonparametric calibration sample at 1,000–2,000 rows. Zygmunt Zając, [Adversarial validation, part one](https://fastml.com/adversarial-validation-part-one/) and [part two](https://fastml.com/adversarial-validation-part-two/): a classifier that separates train from test at AUC≈0.5 means ordinary CV is trustworthy; if it separates them, build the validation set from the train rows that look like test. The Kaggle-master slides at Dauphine, [lec-validation.pdf](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~ebenhamou/Becoming_a_Kaggle_Master/static/slides/lec-validation.pdf), and the [shake-up handbook](https://medium.com/global-maksimum-data-information-technologies/kaggle-handbook-tips-tricks-to-survive-a-kaggle-shake-up-23675beed05e) are the same rule. IEEE-CIS fraud, [41st, written to avoid dropping 1,000 places](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/ieee-fraud-detection/writeups/lets-try-not-to-drop-1000-places-41st-place-soluti): they dropped features a train-vs-test model could score above 0.60 AUC, and their blend of "all features" with "stable features" is what survived.

**What they did.** They treated post-hoc calibration and public-board weight tuning as procedures that need more held-out rows than a datathon has, and they used a train-versus-test classifier only as a diagnostic.

**Map.** g4: v6's win-probability deciles sit within ±0.02. Validation is a handful of episodes per condition, far below the 100-row break-even, and the labels in `labels` are one sampled outcome rather than the true distribution in `targets.csv`. Isotonic regression on those outcomes would fit noise and move a calibrated forecast. Stacking on the same validation episodes is how g3's blends went to zero or below. Adversarial validation *is* useful Sunday, in a cheap form: after `adapt()` prints pairs, confirm the new train and the new test are not separable on crude features (history length, empirical win rate, gap size). If they are, the mask in idea 1 is the wrong mask.

**Do this.** Add a line to the runbook: no Platt, no beta, no isotonic, no temperature fit on validation. A temperature on the size mixture is allowed only if it is chosen on train halves (it is one of idea 5's knobs, not a calibration layer). **Round-two safe.** The ban is safe. **Cost.** Zero. **Expected gain.** Zero on the main board, and it removes a failure mode that the Quantix study measured at several points of Brier on small sets. Our score is a Brier against the true probabilities, so a calibration layer that chases sampled labels is aimed at the wrong target anyway.

### 8. Jackpot live: shrink toward the machine's own rate only when the between-machine spread says the type mean is a poor guide

**Sources.** Loss Data Analytics, [chapter 12](https://openacttexts.github.io/LDAVer2/ChapCredibility.html): Bühlmann credibility is the linear estimator Z·(machine mean) + (1−Z)·(type mean), with Z = n/(n+K) and K = (expected process variance) / (variance of hypothetical means), both estimated from the portfolio. The SOA study note [Topics in Credibility Theory](https://www.soa.org/globalassets/assets/Files/Edu/2018/2018-stam-23-18.pdf) gives the moment estimators. CAS, [credibility as data augmentation](https://forum.casact.org/article/132224-credibility-as-data-augmentation): K is a pseudocount, which is the form v6 already uses, but the pseudocount is estimated from the portfolio rather than typed in. Cross Validated, [method of moments for beta-binomial EB](https://stats.stackexchange.com/questions/320501/empirical-bayes-method-of-moments), [the same derivation with a worked posterior mean](https://stats.stackexchange.com/questions/313579/hyper-parameter-estimation-for-beta-binomial-empirical-bayes), and [what add-one actually assumes](https://stats.stackexchange.com/questions/305267/probability-with-limited-data): add-one is Beta(1,1); a type with a tight cluster of rates wants a large pseudocount, a type whose rates scatter wants a small one. Casella's moments make that automatic, and they **do not exist** if the sample variance is below the binomial floor, in which case the honest output is "full pooling."

**What they did.** They set the strength of the prior from the spread across parallel risks, per class, and they pooled completely when the spread was indistinguishable from noise.

**Map.** g4: jackpot live is about half of all win-probability error, truth 0.58±0.16, v6 explains ~30% of that variance, RMSE 0.137. g1 reads the centre near 0.50–0.58; h1 reads the mean of the targets at 0.674 and a moment-matched global strength at 56.32 versus 56.28, with jackpot slightly worse. Those can both be true if the distribution is skewed: squared error wants the mean, and a global widening was tried. v6's win head is already a credibility formula (posterior mean of a Beta mixture). What h1 did not do is estimate K **inside jackpot, from the variance of per-machine live-cell rates across train**, and then apply that K only on machines whose history is long enough for the machine mean to be a mean. Steady and balanced look tight (g4: v6 is already close there), so a single strength for all three types is the wrong instrument. If jackpot's VHM is large, K is small and the machine's own live rate should move the forecast; if the moment estimator hits the binomial floor, leave the type mean alone.

**Do this.** On train, and only on ticks the fast expert calls live, compute per-machine win rates for jackpot machines with at least n_min such ticks. Estimate K by the Bühlmann moment ratio. Cross-fit the resulting predictive mean against v6's jackpot-live probability, both halves, stable and long histories only. If either half is negative, drop it. Do not touch steady or balanced.

**Round-two safe.** Yes. K is re-estimated; if round two has few jackpot machines the moment estimate falls back to full pooling, which is v6. **Cost.** A few hours. **Expected gain.** The ceiling, if half of the jackpot win-probability gap closed, is on the order of **+0.3 to +0.5** (jackpot is about half of win-probability error, and perfect win probability is +2.15). h1's result says a global version of this is worth ~0. A gated, jackpot-only version is the part not yet measured. Treat +0.1 as the planning number until a cross-fit says otherwise.

### 9. Add one expert that literally starts at the last break, as a peer of the three half-lives

**Sources.** Same Santa 2020 decay note as idea 2, plus the forecast-combination advice in idea 4: a new expert is allowed if it is a different forecast, and its weight should be learned by the same online rule as the others rather than by a stacker. Georg Streich, [2nd, Rock-Paper-Scissors](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rock-paper-scissors/writeups/georg-streich-2nd-place-solution): pick a *group* that has been good for a while, then a member that has been good for the last few moves, and draw the scores from a distribution so one noisy streak does not lock the choice. He reports that more complicated selectors lost to this. Lucas Schnack, [meta-strategy write-up](https://schnack.dev/blog/kaggle/rock-paper-scissors-ensemble-meta-strategy/): a decayed score over selector hyperparameters, and an explicit refuse-to-predict (play uniform) when the weights are indecisive. He also found that removing base policies but keeping their counters got stronger, which he reads as "the ensemble was being distracted by correlated copies." Taaha Khan, [4th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rock-paper-scissors/writeups/taaha-khan-4th-place-solution-taaha-khan): when one head is exploited, another head is already in the pool. The [RPS.bot opponent-modeling tutorial](https://rps.bot/rpsomtutorial.html) is the small worked example of why a policy that never forgets a dead opponent loses the next match.

**What they did.** They kept the pool small and diverse, scored members on more than one timescale, and refused to commit when the scores were flat.

**Map.** Half-lives 12, 40 and 100 are three correlated copies of one idea, which is Schnack's "correlated copies" failure if a fourth half-life is added. A hard-reset expert is not correlated with them in the one regime that matters: after a shared flip it has no pre-break counts, and they do. v6's multiplicative weights already implement Streich's "recent plus longer window" selection. The missing member is the one with a run length, not a fourth decay. Schnack's indecisive-uniform rule is the wrong fallback here (the type prior is much better than uniform) but the diagnostic is right: if the three half-lives disagree with the post-break expert by more than a train-fit margin, output the type prior's win probability instead of the average. That is a confidence floor, and it is aimed at shared_change and at the first ticks after a flip inside a short history.

**Do this.** Only in combination with idea 2's vote, so the new expert is usually identical to the slow one and only splits off after a peer vote. Give it the same `eta` as the others and let the multiplicative weights pick it up. If, on train, its weight never wins a cross-fit, delete it. Do not add the RPS n-gram tree; g3 already showed generic sequence models lose under squared error.

**Round-two safe.** Yes. **Cost.** Small if idea 2 exists. **Expected gain.** Included in idea 2's +0.05 to +0.20. Listed separately because the implementation that failed before (Fixed-Share, a continuous leak of weight) is not this one (a new expert with empty pre-break statistics).

### 10. Pool short series at the type, not at the machine, and average the two forecasts with a weight the data estimates once

**Sources.** Semenoglou, Spiliotis and Makridakis, [cross-learning](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2020.11.009): short series are forecast better when a single model sees many of them. The Monash working paper [Locality and globality](https://www.monash.edu/business/ebs/research/publications/ebs/wp45-2020.pdf) (Januschowski et al. / Monash): a local model overfits a short series; a global model is biased when series differ; the practical winner in M4 was global parameters plus local state, which is Smyl. Smyl's [M4 slides](https://isf.forecasters.org/wp-content/uploads/gravity_forms/2-dd30f7ae09136fa695c552259bdb3f99/2019/06/M4.pdf) and [paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334556784_A_hybrid_method_of_exponential_smoothing_and_recurrent_neural_networks_for_time_series_forecasting): per-series smoothing coefficients, shared network weights, and an ensemble of specialists trained on subsets. Chen, Boylan and Svetunkov, [Foresight on vector exponential smoothing](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/221439/2/Foresight70_Cross-Learning.pdf): for seasonal series too short to estimate a seasonal index, share the index across the group and keep the level local. M5's winner (idea 6) is the same design one level up: store, store-category, and store-department models, equal-weighted. Dan Weitzenfeld, [1st, M6 forecasting](http://danielweitzenfeld.github.io/passtheroc/posts/m6.html), and the [paper](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v41y2025i4p1477-1484.html): hierarchical priors per asset class, heavier weight on the recent past, and a posterior mean obtained by averaging samples. His margin over 2nd and 3rd was 0.00003–0.00004 of ranked probability score ([3rd-place repo](https://github.com/sebrad/M6) prints the table: 0.15645 / 0.15648 / 0.15649). He treats that as too small to crown a method.

**What they did.** They shared the parts of the model the group can estimate (seasonal shape, class variance, a network) and kept the level local. They did not fit a different functional form per series. The M6 podium is a warning about declaring victory inside the noise.

**Map.** v6 is already this architecture: type-level size prior and win-prior components, machine-level counts. The missing share is across the **8 machines of one episode** for the size atom's base rate when each machine has few wins, and across **jackpot machines in the train portfolio** for K (idea 8). A new global neural net is what g3's GRU result says not to build. The M6 margin is the right comparison for h1's +0.04 and for any future +0.05.

**Do this.** No new model. When idea 5 looks up a type histogram, compute it from machines of that type in the same episode if the episode is long, and from the train portfolio if the episode is short. That is the only new pooling. Equal-weight it with the portfolio histogram rather than fitting a third coefficient (M5, and the combination puzzle). Judge the result by both halves. If the two halves disagree in sign, the M6 lesson applies: the difference is noise, ship v6.

**Round-two safe.** Yes. **Cost.** Small, and only as part of idea 5. **Expected gain.** Inside idea 5's range. The standalone value of this item is the decision rule: a gain that is not positive on both halves is not a gain.

## What this literature says not to do

These are practitioner results, not a restatement of FINDINGS, except where a write-up explains a loss we already measured.

- **Another sequence model** (n-gram tree, GRU, Kalman-on-the-raw-payouts). Web Traffic's 1st and 8th won by putting a simple seasonal structure in front of the flexible model. Hungry Geese's [1st place](https://engineering.dena.com/blog/2021/12/distributed-reinforcement-learning-with-handyrl/) won with search on top of a learned value function because the *simulator was known and callable*. Ours is known only up to a small rule class; the matching move is idea 3, not an RL agent. Lux AI Season 1 is the cautionary twin: a [hand-written heuristic](https://michaelriedl.com/posts/luxai-s01/) that ignored the actual resolution order in the [spec](https://www.lux-ai.org/specs-2021) stalled around the middle of the board.
- **Post-hoc calibration** on validation (idea 7).
- **Unshrunk blend weights**, negative weights, and fine hill-climbing (idea 4). g3 already measured the unshrunk version.
- **Fixed-Share and any detector that fires on stable episodes** (idea 2). The continuous-adaptation paper [arxiv 1810.02266](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.02266) is the academic version of the same warning: a reset on a false alarm throws away a good model. Drift, where v6 is already best, is where a reset does that.
- **Chasing a sub-0.4 gain the way M6's top three sat 0.00004 apart.** h1's +0.04 is in that band.
- **Pseudolabels from sampled outcomes.** Forbes's crypto result is the closest: a target that changes meaning when rows are missing will be learned as two problems. Our `labels` are one draw from the distribution we are scored against. Training on hard labels is already what `cutoff_brier` does inside `adapt()`; it is a noisy proxy for `targets.csv` and should not be replaced by the model's own previous predictions.
- **A global retune of win-prior centres.** h1 did the moment-matched version and it tied. Idea 8 is deliberately narrower.

## Recommendation

Monday's main upload stays the 56.29 zip unless a cross-fit of idea 1 clears +0.4 or a paired interval above 0. The order of experiments, each gated on both episode halves:

1. `decay_missing=False` on short-history episodes. Already in the code. If it wins, it is the whole of idea 1(a).
2. A masked refit of half-lives and the two size pseudocounts (idea 1(b)), dispatched on the gap pattern.
3. In parallel, g5's size-rule list scored as a Brier average of the near-ties (idea 3), not as a single plugged-in rule.
4. Peer-vote reset of the slow expert (ideas 2 and 9) only if shared_change improves and stable does not move.
5. The count floor on the size head (ideas 5 and 10) if idea 3 has not found a rule.
6. Jackpot-only Bühlmann K (idea 8) last, and only on long stable histories.

The filter-versus-v6 selector (idea 4) is worth finishing because g6 is already running it, and it should ship only under the shrinkage rule. Its realistic ceiling is about +0.1, under the noise, so it is not a reason to delay the upload. Idea 6's runbook is the Sunday plan regardless of which of the above survive: `round2_shift` rehearsal, freeze knobs, refit on all of the new train, abort to the previous zip on the clock, and no calibration layer.

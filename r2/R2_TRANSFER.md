# Round-two transfer test: v13 on a simulated shifted round

Question: when round two changes which lags drive the machines, does v13
re-learn the new structure from train history, or does it stay anchored to
the main-round lags (1,3), (2,5), (4,7)?

## Setup

- Data: `r2/gen_round2_shift` (preset `round2_shift`) and `r2/gen_main_like`
  (preset `main_like`), generated by `mainFiles/eval_suite/generator.py`
  with 24 episodes per condition for each split.
- Kit-shaped inputs: `r2/data_round2_shift` and `r2/data_main_like` are copies
  of the generator folders with `_truth/` removed (the `_truth` folder sat
  beside the splits, so it was excluded from the copy).
- Scoring: `mainFiles/eval_suite/scoring.py::official_score` against
  `_truth/test` targets, conditions and reference, on the TEST split.
  Round two uses max 40 points (`metadata.json`), main-like uses 60.
  Script: `r2/score_r2.py`.
- Sanity check: the pooled starter scores exactly 20.0 (R = 1.0) on the
  shifted test, so `_truth/test/reference.csv` is the pooled starter's output.

## Results (TEST split, generated truth)

| Model | Preset | Points | R | stable | short_history | drift | shared_change | Runtime (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pooled starter (`run_starter.py --method pooled`) | round2_shift | 20.00 / 40 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 4 s |
| v13 (`run_strongest.py`) | round2_shift | **25.22 / 40** | 0.586 | 0.309 | 0.724 | 0.875 | 0.436 | about 4 min |
| v13 (`run_strongest.py`) | main_like (reference) | 38.97 / 60 | 0.540 | 0.262 | 0.805 | 0.706 | 0.386 | about 8 min |

Ratios are per-condition E/B (lower is better). Runtime is wall time from file
timestamps, with both v13 runs sharing the CPU and no clean timing captured.
Its stdout log came back empty, so the learned-structure readout below comes
from calling the entry's own discovery functions (`choose_pairs`,
`discover_size_keys`) on each train split.

The v13 validation self-score (`validation_score.json`) agrees in direction:
technical points 25.35 / 40 on the shifted round and 41.30 / 60 on main-like.

## Learned vs true structure

Generator `round2_shift` (`eval_suite/generator.py`, `preset_config`):
recurring lags = **(2, 3, 5, 7)**, interaction strength 0.85, shared change
0.55. Each machine's interaction pairs, neighbour lag and size lag are drawn
only from these four lags.

v13 discovery on the shifted TRAIN split (`pair_mixer.choose_pairs`):
pairs found (2,3), (2,5), (2,7), (3,5), (3,7), (5,7). That is every pair of
the true lag set, with no lag 1, 4, 6 or 8 chosen. The per-machine top pairs
were (3,5) 102, (3,7) 97, (5,7) 96, (2,5) 88, (2,7) 76, (2,3) 71, with only
(3,6) 23 and (4,7) 15 as stray hits.

Size keys (`discover_size_keys`) on TRAIN: (2, 3, 5, 7), exactly the true size
lags. On VALIDATION they came out (2, 3, 4, 5), so one key is wrong and lag 7
is missed. The validation pair set also drifts slightly.

Main-like check: the generator uses lags 1..8, and v13's train discovery
returns all 28 pairs of lags 1..8 with no dominant pair. This matches the
diffuse structure in that preset, and the model's fixed main-round pairs
(1,3), (2,5), (4,7) are not picked on the shifted data.

## Verdict

v13 re-learns the shifted structure from train history alone. The lag set
(2,3,5,7) is recovered, and it beats the pooled starter by 5.2 points
(R 0.59 vs 1.00). The gain is largest on stable and shared_change, where the
per-machine lag rules matter most. Drift (0.88) is the weakest condition and
the one where it gains least. Caveat: this is one simulated round with 24
episodes per condition, not the real round two. Check the lag screen again
on the real round-two train data before trusting it.
